Brand setup is a one-time task. Every piece of copy the Writer produces and every visual the Designer creates will reference the settings you configure here — without you repeating yourself on each post. Take 15–20 minutes to complete this section thoroughly and the agents will sound and look like your brand from the very first draft.

<Tip>
  Upload 5–10 of your best past posts as tone examples during the Tone of Voice step. The more concrete examples the Writer sees, the less you will need to edit its output later.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Brand settings apply workspace-wide — all agents across all channels use them by default. If you need a channel to deviate (for example, a more formal tone on LinkedIn than on TikTok), you can set channel-level overrides in **Settings → Channels → [Channel name] → Brand Overrides**.
</Note>

## 1. Tone of Voice

Navigate to **Settings → Brand → Tone of Voice**.

You have two ways to teach the Writer your brand voice:

- **Upload past posts** — paste in or upload 5–10 examples of content you have already published and are happy with. The Writer reads them to extract your vocabulary, sentence structure, and implicit style rules.
- **Write a description** — describe how your brand sounds in plain language. For example: "Friendly and direct. We speak to Gulf SMB owners. We avoid corporate jargon and never use superlatives."

Then configure the following:

- **Language and dialect** — choose Arabic (Modern Standard), Arabic (Gulf dialect), English, or a combination. You can also set a default language per channel.
- **Formality level** — select from Casual, Neutral, or Formal. This affects vocabulary choice and sentence length across all generated copy.

## 2. Prohibited Words & Promises

Navigate to **Settings → Brand → Prohibited Words**.

List any words, phrases, or marketing claims the agents must never include in your content. Common entries include:

- Competitor brand names you do not want to mention
- Superlatives your legal or compliance team has flagged (e.g., "best in the world," "guaranteed")
- Internal jargon that should never be customer-facing
- Specific product claims that have not been approved

The Writer treats this list as a hard constraint. If a draft contains a prohibited phrase, it is automatically rewritten before it reaches your calendar.

## 3. Brand Assets

Navigate to **Settings → Brand → Assets**.

Upload the visual building blocks your brand uses:

- **Logo** — upload your primary logo file (SVG or PNG with a transparent background preferred). The Designer places it on every image and video it creates.
- **Brand colors** — set your primary, secondary, and accent colors as hex codes. The Designer uses these for backgrounds, text overlays, and graphic elements.
- **Fonts** — upload your brand typeface files (TTF or OTF). The Designer burns your brand font into all generated visuals, including video captions.

Once these are saved, the Designer agent requires no further instruction to stay on brand. You will not need to specify colors or fonts on individual posts.

## 4. Media Library

Navigate to **Settings → Brand → Media Library**.

Upload the photos, videos, and design assets you own and want the Designer to use. Examples include:

- Product photography
- Behind-the-scenes footage
- Team photos
- Raw video clips for short-form content

The Designer always checks your media library first before generating anything new. If you have a suitable asset, it uses that. AI generation is a fallback, not a default. Keeping your library stocked with fresh assets gives every post an authentic, on-brand look without relying on generated visuals.
